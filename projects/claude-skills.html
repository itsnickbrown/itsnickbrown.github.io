Why skills

Most of a channel manager's week repeats: read the POS, vet an applicant, write the escalation, build the account plan, send the weekly report. A good prompt helps once. A skill helps every time, because it holds the steps, the format and the judgment calls I would otherwise retype.

What I built

  • pos-analysis: reads distributor POS, flags spikes, drops and gaps, leads with the "so what" and compares YoY and QoQ.
  • partner-vetting: qualifying questions, red flags and an approve, hold or decline call with a one-line reason. Try it below.
  • account-planning, weekly-activity-report, escalation-email, contact-finder, cold-email, copy-editing, sales-enablement.
  • A branded slide skill for QBR decks, and writing-style skills that strip filler and hype.

A context file loads at the start of every session: the channel model, the distributors, my accounts, current priorities and my writing rules. I stopped re-explaining my job to the model.

Connectors brought in live data. A ZoomInfo connection enriched about 230 contacts across 95 accounts. I also vetted a set of public skills and passed them to my manager for the team.

How I work with it

I treat the model like a sharp new hire. I give it context and rules, I review its work, and I check numbers against the source before anything goes out. When it gets something wrong twice, the fix goes into the skill, not into my memory.
